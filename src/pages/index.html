Tumor evolution through therapy in an Acute Myeloid Leukemia

About me

My work centers on using large-scale genomic data and custom computational methods to understand how cancers arise, how their subclonal populations evolve, and how they respond to therapy, with a particular focus on acute myeloid leukemia.

Core interests:

  • Studying the genomic and epigenomic alterations that drive tumorigenesis
  • The clonal architecture of tumors and how it evolves in response to therapy
  • Multi-omics integration to understand the complex pathways driving cancer
  • Effective design and targeting of cancer immunotherapies
  • Developing open-source software for integrating, mining, and visualizing genomic data
  • Educating the next generation of computationally-savvy scientists

Before moving to WashU, I completed a PhD in Computational Biology at Baylor College of Medicine. My thesis was about using bioinformatic techniques and large-scale data integration to help understand the aetiology of cancer.
